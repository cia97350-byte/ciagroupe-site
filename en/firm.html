The firm

A project-engineering practice
in French Guiana, for its region.

CIA — Conseil, Ingénierie et Assistance — supports public bodies, companies and investors across the whole life of a project: the study that founds it, the funding that makes it possible, the procedure that secures it, and the delivery that brings it into being.

Our method

I

Listen. A thirty-minute call. We listen, we qualify, and we tell you whether the project stands up. If it does not, we say so there and then.

II

Assess. A written note, costed and sourced. Every regulatory statement is verified as at the date of the report, with its reference.

III

Build. The complete file: documents, budgets, procedure, timetable, contacts. We carry the file; we do not comment on it.

IV

Support. Follow-through to the outcome and beyond: delivery, compliance, regulatory watch.

Our principles

Four rules we do not negotiate.

  • The source before the opinion. We source everything we assert: an article of law, a verification date. A figure without a source does not enter one of our reports.
  • Saying no is part of the service. A file we do not believe fundable, we say so before being paid to build it. A study that always concludes in favour is worth nothing.
  • Each to their own trade. We provide engineering and coordination. Legal, accounting and notarial work is carried out by professionals on the French registers, whom we mobilise and whose timetable we hold.
  • Substance before tax advantage. No arrangement without real operations. That is a firm rule, not a drafting precaution.

The group

We know the real costs because we carry them.

CIA is the engineering arm of a set of companies operating in French Guiana in private security and remote monitoring, waste collection and logistics, vocational training, energy and agri-processing.

That position is the core of our value: we know the real cost of a hire, the real lead time of a shipment and the real weight of octroi de mer, because we carry them ourselves every month.
